IndexRiskNext steps
Below 130 Low Horses can generally cool themselves normally. Regular riding and tours may continue with normal hydration and cooldown procedures.
130–150 Moderate Monitor horses closely for signs of heat stress. Increase water breaks and recovery time.
150–180 High Cooling ability is significantly reduced. Shorten tours, reduce workload, increase cooling measures, and monitor horses and guests constantly.
Above 180 Extreme Serious risk of heat stress and heat stroke. Consider postponing, canceling, or rescheduling tours.
Guidelines only — leadership evaluates all factors and makes the operational call. Report any horse or guest showing heat-stress signs (panting, no sweat, fatigue, dizziness, confusion) to leadership immediately.
